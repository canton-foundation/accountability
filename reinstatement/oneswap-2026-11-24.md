## 1. What happened

The pools under review were used by designated market makers operating under OneSwap's market-making arrangements.

### 1.1 Designated market-maker activity

OneSwap maintained certain pools with access restricted to whitelisted market makers. These restrictions were intended to protect liquidity providers and token markets from extractive arbitrage activity and other behaviour that could harm liquidity.

The pools allowed several designated market makers to conduct market-making and liquidity-management activity in a controlled environment. The activity included trading associated with liquidity, inventory and market operations. The participants were identifiable market-making counterparties.

The purpose of the restricted access was to:

* Allow designated market makers to provide liquidity and support orderly markets.
* Protect liquidity providers and token markets from extractive arbitrage strategies.
* Allow market makers to manage liquidity and inventory in the relevant markets.
* Protect transaction privacy, as information about activity in public pools is more visible and can be easier to monitor and manipulate.
* Support market-making activity while limiting access by participants whose conduct could harm liquidity providers or tokens.

### 1.2 Context for the activity

The restricted pools were not public retail markets. They were access-controlled pools used by designated market makers for market-making and liquidity-management activity. Restricted access also provided greater privacy for transactions, since activity and pool information in public pools is more visible and can be easier to monitor and manipulate.

OneSwap asks FAAC and Accountability to assess the activity on that basis, rather than treating restricted access, a low number of counterparties or the absence of an individual on-ledger fee as conclusive evidence that the activity was artificial or lacked an economic purpose.

## 3. What changes going forward

OneSwap has changed its pool-access policy from whitelist-only access to open access with moderation and targeted blacklisting. This is intended to preserve legitimate market-making activity while addressing the risks that led to the earlier restricted-access model.

* **Pools open to participants.** Since 19 Nov 2026 every active pool on OneSwap is open and there are no allowlisted pools. Moderation and targeted blacklisting are used to address harmful activity.
* **Moderation and blacklisting.** Accounts engaging in extractive arbitrage or otherwise harming liquidity providers or tokens will be identified and blocked, rather than limiting access for all users except a whitelist of approved participants.
* **Designated moderators.** OneSwap has assigned new moderators for Canton. Internal resources are being moved from other blockchains on which OneSwap operates to Canton, reflecting Canton’s priority for OneSwap.
* **Supporting TVL and trading-volume growth.** Designated market makers will continue to be able to participate in open pools and conduct market-making activity, supporting the growth of token TVL and trading volume on Canton for both market makers and retail users. The policy change concerns how access is governed, not the removal of market-making activity.
* **Vetted Trader SDK keys and API access.** Trader SDK keys are issued only after the integrator has been vetted. Nobody can create one themselves. OneSwap is also working on tiered API access so that pool information is used responsibly. Certain private information exposed through the API will be available only to whitelisted parties, preserving transaction and pool privacy.
* **Monitoring.** We check activity by product every day in our per-transaction reward ledger.

### 3.1 Automatic controls

OneSwap is putting the following automatic controls in place so that this kind of activity cannot recur unless someone deliberately overrides them:

| Control | Trigger | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Concentration alert | Every 15 minutes, OneSwap measures the top two parties' share of each pool's 24-hour volume. Triggers above 50%. | Moderators are alerted and review the pool the same day. |
| Automatic marker stop | Top two parties' share of a pool's 24-hour volume above 80%. | Marking for that pool stops automatically until a moderator clears it and records a reason. |
| No allowlisted pools | Any attempt to create or change a pool with an access allowlist. | The request is rejected. Access can be limited only by blacklisting individual parties. |

We ask FAAC to consider the identity and role of the market makers, the commercial arrangements and the purpose of the activity when reviewing the public-ledger figures, and to restore OneSwap's Asset-Moving designation.
